01 — About us

One program where neuroscience, engineering and the clinic finally share a bench.

Neuromodulation-based therapies are moving faster than any single discipline can follow. GAINN gives graduate trainees integrated training that bridges the clinical and engineering dimensions of the field, alongside exposure to the patients, operating rooms and emerging technologies shaping brain health research.

The program is delivered through the CRANIA NeuroModulation Institute at the University of Toronto, with research and clinical placements hosted across its partner hospitals.

01

Cross-disciplinary coursework

Graduate courses and workshops drawn from engineering, neuroscience and medicine, taught by neuromodulation researchers and practising clinicians.

02

Clinical immersion

Placements inside hospital environments, so trainees see how stimulation therapies are actually delivered, monitored and adjusted for patients.

03

Translation and industry

Professional skills, entrepreneurship and industry partnership training aimed at moving neurotechnology from the lab toward approved clinical use.

02 — The field

The modalities trainees work across

Implantable and non-implantable technologies that interface with the nervous system — and the computation that makes sense of what comes back.

M01

Deep brain stimulation

Implanted electrodes delivering targeted stimulation to subcortical structures.

M02

Spinal and peripheral stimulation

Interfaces with the spinal cord and peripheral nerves for movement and pain.

M03

Non-invasive stimulation

Stimulation delivered through the skull, without an implanted device.

M04

Optical interfaces and optogenetics

Light used to activate or inhibit specific neurons with high precision.

M05

Brain–computer interfaces

Decoding neural activity into control signals for devices and prosthetics.

M06

Machine learning and big data

Signal analysis and models that turn recorded activity into therapy decisions.
